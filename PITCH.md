# MMRY — pitch deck structure

Slide-by-slide outline. For each slide: what goes on it, and what you say.

**Anything in `[BRACKETS]` is a number or fact you must find yourself.** They are
left blank on purpose — invented figures are the fastest way to lose a room, and
one wrong number makes everything else you said suspect.

The running order assumes the **friend's walk** leads (see `NOTES.md` §3). If you
decide the time capsule or institutional route leads instead, slides 2, 3 and 8
change; the rest holds.

---

## Before anything else: solve the demo problem

**This product cannot be demonstrated sitting down.** It needs GPS movement, and
you will be pitching in a room. This is the single biggest practical risk in your
pitch and most people discover it on the day.

Fix it in advance, in this order:

1. **Record a screen-capture video of a real walk** — the map with your dot
   moving, the zone circle turning orange, and the audio coming in. Two minutes,
   no narration, real footage. This is your most valuable asset and you can shoot
   it this week.
2. **Bring headphones and the phone.** Hand it over during Q&A so they hold the
   real thing, even standing still.
3. **If the venue allows it, pitch outside or take them out afterwards.** A
   ninety-second walk beats any slide you will ever make.

Do not rely on live GPS in a room. Concrete, mineral-heavy buildings will
ruin it.

---

## Slide 1 — Title

**On the slide:** MMRY. One line underneath describing what it is.

Try: *"Music and memory, tied to places."* Keep it under ten words, no jargon.

**You say:** your name, and that you'll show them something that works before
you explain it.

---

## Slide 2 — The moment

Open with the experience, not the market. Make them feel it before you explain
anything.

**On the slide:** one photograph of a real place. Ideally one of your zones.

**You say:** Describe someone walking toward a spot and music beginning — not
because they pressed play, but because they arrived. Twenty seconds. No
technology words yet.

The instinct to resist: opening with "the audio tourism market is worth X."
Nobody has ever been moved by that sentence.

---

## Slide 3 — The demo

**This is your best slide. Do not leave it until the end.**

**On the slide:** the video from above, playing.

**You say:** almost nothing. Let it run. Then: *"Everything you just saw is
live. You can walk it today."*

Then give the URL and let someone open it on their own phone.

---

## Slide 4 — Why this hasn't happened already

**On the slide:** three or four bullets on what changed.

Fill these in honestly — this slide is weak if the answer is just "phones got
better." Candidates worth checking:

- `[SMARTPHONE GPS ACCURACY / PENETRATION IN YOUR TARGET MARKET]`
- Wireless earbuds moved from rare to ordinary — audio-first products now have
  an audience that is already wearing the hardware
- `[AUDIO CONTENT CREATION COSTS — what does recording/editing cost now vs 2015]`
- People make and share voice notes constantly now; recording yourself is no
  longer strange

**You say:** why the timing is right *now* rather than five years ago or five
years from now.

---

## Slide 5 — How it works

**On the slide:** a simple diagram. Place → radius → audio. Three icons.

**You say:** You drop a point on a map, attach a sound, set how close someone
must be. That's the whole system. Then the important part: *"the same engine
serves three completely different markets."*

Keep it short. They do not care how it works; they care that it does, and they
just watched it.

---

## Slide 6 — One engine, three markets

**On the slide:** the table from `NOTES.md` §2.

| | Who makes it | Who pays |
|---|---|---|
| A walk made for someone you know | You | — |
| Museums, cities, festivals | Institutions | Institutions |
| Time capsule, unlocked years later | You | You, upfront |

**You say:** these are not three products, they are three audiences for one
tool. Then immediately move to sequencing — do not let this land as
unfocused.

---

## Slide 7 — The order, and why

**On the slide:** the three, numbered, with one line of reasoning each.

**You say:**

1. **Friend's walks first.** Cheapest to build, no rights negotiations, and
   distribution is built into the product — every walk created arrives with at
   least one person motivated to install it.
2. **Institutions second.** Identical tool, sold rather than posted. This is
   where budgets exist.
3. **Time capsule last.** Best story, hardest promise — it needs a company that
   looks permanent.

This slide is what turns three ideas into a strategy. Rehearse it.

---

## Slide 8 — Why it's different

Every audio walking product is *earnest*. Reverent museum voice, professional
narrator, historical significance.

**On the slide:** a contrast. Their tone versus yours.

**You say:** the differentiator is that a walk from a friend can be funny,
rude, personal, stupid. *"Here's the giant forehead of some guy who died"* is a
sentence no existing product will ever contain. That tone is an unoccupied
position and it's the reason someone forwards it.

---

## Slide 9 — The Detour question

**Put this in the deck. Do not wait to be asked.**

Raising a known failure yourself signals you've done the work. Being caught not
knowing about it signals the opposite.

**On the slide:** Detour, 2015–2018, acquired by Bose. One line on what killed
it: App Store invisibility.

**You say:** the full answer is written out in `NOTES.md` §7. The core of it:
Detour was a catalogue a stranger had to go find, and it ranked in the 400s–700s
of the Travel category. MMRY arrives as a message from someone you know. That's
a distribution model, not a growth tactic.

Also worth saying out loud: the tooling Detour built internally became Descript,
which outgrew it. You know that story and you've thought about what it means.

---

## Slide 10 — Market

**Everything here is `[TO RESEARCH]`.** Do not guess. Do not use a number you
cannot source in the room.

Worth finding:

- `[LATVIAN / BALTIC TOURISM VISITOR NUMBERS]` — Central Statistical Bureau of
  Latvia publishes these
- `[MUSEUM AND HERITAGE AUDIO GUIDE SPEND]` — check what museums currently pay
  for handset guides; this is your institutional wedge
- `[COMPARABLE PRICING]` — what VoiceMap and izi.TRAVEL charge per tour, and
  what their institutional deals look like
- `[SIZE OF THE GIFTING / EXPERIENCE GIFT MARKET]` if the friend's walk leads

Prefer a small number you can defend to a large one you cannot.

---

## Slide 11 — Business model

**On the slide:** how money arrives, per market.

- Friend's walks: `[FREE? PAID? WHO PAYS — the maker or the walker?]`
- Institutions: per-project fee, or licence — `[DECIDE WHICH]`
- Time capsule: one payment at sealing, covering storage

**You say:** be honest that the first is the audience and the second is the
revenue. Investors respect a founder who knows which part is the business.

---

## Slide 12 — What already exists

**On the slide:** screenshots. What is built and working *today*.

- Live GPS triggering, walked and verified on real hardware
- A builder where anyone makes their own journey — no coding
- Deployed and publicly reachable
- Built for `[YOUR TIME INVESTED]` and zero cash

**You say:** this is the strongest slide most early founders don't have. You
are not pitching an idea. It exists and it works.

---

## Slide 13 — Roadmap and what's needed

**On the slide:** the phases from `NOTES.md` §4, compressed to one line each.

Be straight about the current limits — they are in `NOTES.md` §1. A browser
cannot track with the screen off; that's what forces a native app. Naming your
own product's limits before an investor finds them is a credibility play, and it
works.

**You say:** what the money is actually for. `[AMOUNT AND WHAT IT BUYS]`

---

## Slide 14 — Team

`[WHO YOU ARE. WHY YOU.]`

If it's just you, say so plainly and say what you're missing. "I need someone
who can sell to museums" is a stronger sentence than pretending you have it
covered.

---

## Slide 15 — The ask

**On the slide:** one number, one sentence on what it buys, one timeline.

**You say:** exactly what you want from this room — money, an introduction, a
pilot customer, permission to run a walk at their festival. Vague asks get
vague answers.

---

## Things to prepare that aren't slides

- **The video.** Highest-value item on this list. Shoot it this week.
- **Your phone, charged, with the demo open and headphones ready.**
- **A backup if the venue has no signal** — the video covers you.
- **An answer to "what stops Spotify or Google doing this?"** You will be asked.
  Think about it before you're standing there.
- **An answer to "why would anyone make a walk?"** This is the real weakness of
  the friend's-walk model and the sharpest question in the room.
